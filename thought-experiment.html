The documents increase in depth. Start with the one-page brief for the central findings, then follow the question you want to investigate: policy design (2), long-run spending (3), or evidence and methodology (4). You can read them individually or in order.

How to interpret the financial comparisons

The study compares coverage and payment rules under four scenarios:

AExisting coverage
Existing prices
BUniversal coverage
Existing prices
CExisting coverage
Regulated prices
DUniversal coverage
Regulated prices

D minus A is the principal spending comparison. A positive value means higher modeled national healthcare spending than business as usual; a negative value means lower modeled spending. These are all-payer national totals, not estimates of additional federal borrowing.

Central result: Scenario D is modeled at approximately $15.68 trillion more over 30 years and $314.589 trillion more over 100 years than Scenario A. More aggressive price-setting examples can reverse the arithmetic, but their provider reimbursement assumptions and real-world feasibility are not validated. This work warrants independent testing, not an immediate national launch.

An invitation to independent scrutiny

What would it take to validate this?

We welcome substantive criticism, alternative evidence, and referrals to qualified reviewers.

This is a research proposition, not a ready-to-enact plan. Four evidence gates need defensible answers before implementation could responsibly be considered:

  1. 01

    Provider-safe payment rates

    Can reimbursement meet actual service-delivery costs while keeping practices viable?

  2. 02

    Staffed care capacity

    Can the workforce, facilities, and appointment supply serve the additional patients?

  3. 03

    Financing and transition cash flows

    What must be funded, by whom, and when—especially during the transition?

  4. 04

    Measurable health and financial value

    Will patient outcomes and total system performance improve under observed conditions?

Challenge an assumption. Share evidence. Forward to policy staff.

We are seeking concrete critiques, source corrections, and independent technical review—not an endorsement.

About this research

Prepared by: Dark Star Consulting Group LLC
Research release: October 28, 2026
Status: Independent working paper; not peer reviewed, independently certified, or approved for implementation.

Scope: Hypothetical national models for 2025–2054, with an extended 2025–2124 financial projection. These are modeled years, not a record of a completed rollout.

Research basis: Published evidence and data families including CMS, RAND, Milliman, HCCI, JAMA, and MedPAC. Full references, assumptions, and limitations appear in Document 4.

The proposal models one primary public payer, independent care providers, and 26 benefit areas. Covered care is modeled with $0 payment at the point of service; that does not mean the healthcare system itself has zero cost.
