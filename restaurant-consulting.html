Operational Review

Start by finding the real bottleneck.

An owner may feel the symptoms before the cause is obvious. An operational review is the lower-friction starting point: examine the system, identify what is creating the most drag, and separate expensive noise from the changes that actually deserve attention.

Review areas can include

  • Menu structure, recipe execution, food cost and ingredient utilization
  • Prep, production, station flow and service timing
  • Purchasing, pars, vendor fit and product specifications
  • Training, standards, consistency and role clarity
  • Guest-facing execution and the operational causes behind recurring service issues

The output

A prioritized operating picture: what is working, what is not, where the meaningful risks or leaks are, and what should be addressed first. Scope can stop there or move into implementation.
