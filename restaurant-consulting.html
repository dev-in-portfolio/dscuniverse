01 · Entry Points

New. Operating. Rebuilding.

Restaurant problems change with the stage of the business. The engagement starts where the operation actually is, not with a one-size-fits-all consulting package assembled by someone who enjoys templates more than restaurants.

Opening / Pre-Opening

Build before service exposes the gaps.

Concept alignment, menu structure, cost awareness, kitchen workflow, vendor requirements, standards, training priorities, and opening-readiness planning.

Existing Operation

Find the drag on performance.

Focused review for margin pressure, inconsistent execution, labor or workflow problems, training gaps, menu drift, sourcing issues, or service friction.

Relaunch / Reset

Change direction without carrying every old problem forward.

Operational reset around new menus, concept changes, leadership transitions, kitchen reorganization, standardization, supplier changes, or a broader relaunch.

02 · Operating Capabilities

The restaurant as a system.

Food, labor, equipment, purchasing, prep, training, timing, and guest experience collide every service. Consulting treats those pressures as one operating system rather than a pile of unrelated symptoms.

01

Menu & Concept Strategy

Menu architecture, concept alignment, dish development, seasonal refinement, execution requirements, and decisions grounded in the restaurant's actual operating model.

02

Menu Engineering & Food Cost

Recipe structure, portion discipline, yield awareness, ingredient utilization, pricing context, menu mix, and identifying items whose economics do not match their role.

03

Kitchen & Service Workflow

Prep systems, station flow, production sequencing, pars, handoffs, service timing, sanitation discipline, and practical recommendations for how work moves through the operation.

04

SOPs, Training & Standards

Repeatable procedures, recipe and plating standards, opening and closing systems, role clarity, training materials, accountability points, and consistency controls.

05

Vendor & Supplier Sourcing

Product specifications, supplier comparison, purchasing needs, packaging or equipment sourcing, substitution planning, and client-directed supplier research when the project requires it.

06

Opening & Relaunch Readiness

Operational sequencing, checklists, readiness review, menu and system validation, training priorities, vendor coordination, and identifying gaps before opening pressure makes them expensive.

Operational Diagnostic

A focused diagnostic can be used when the operator knows something is wrong but the source is unclear. The review can examine the menu, cost structure, prep, purchasing, labor deployment, station flow, standards, training, consistency, and service execution, then rank the highest-value corrections.

Output

  • Observed operating friction
  • Priority-ranked findings
  • Immediate corrections
  • Longer-term system work
  • Recommended next engagement, if needed
