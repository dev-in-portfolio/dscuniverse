01 · Entry Points

New. Operating. Rebuilding.

A new restaurant has different pressures than an established operation with declining sales. The engagement starts with what is actually happening: what the owner knows, what the numbers show, and what still needs to be investigated.

Opening / Pre-Opening

Build before service exposes the gaps.

Concept alignment, menu structure, cost awareness, kitchen workflow, vendor requirements, standards, training priorities, and opening-readiness planning.

Operating / Underperforming

Find what is holding the business back.

Investigate sales friction, margins, labor deployment, menu economics, customer reach, workflow, purchasing, and operational consistency before committing to a fix.

Expanding / Relaunching

Grow without carrying old problems forward.

Evaluate new channels, supplier changes, territory opportunities, concept adjustments, repeat-order systems, and the operational capacity needed to support them.

02 · Consulting Capabilities

The restaurant as a business.

A restaurant can execute beautifully and still struggle with location, pricing, customer reach, or purchasing. Operations and commercial performance have to be examined together. Each engagement is scoped to the actual problem; not every project requires every discipline.

01 · Operational Performance

Make the operation work.

Menu structure, food and labor systems, prep, training, service flow, consistency, and opening readiness.

02 · Profitability & Purchasing

Understand the real economics.

Recipe yields, pricing, supplier terms, packaging units, purchasing comparisons, and the costs that accumulate between an order and a guest.

03 · Market & Revenue Growth

Find commercial opportunities.

Location and trade-area research, customer categories, wholesale and hospitality channels, events, regional delivery, and repeat-account strategy.

01

Menu & Concept Strategy

Menu architecture, concept alignment, dish development, seasonal refinement, execution requirements, and decisions grounded in the restaurant's actual operating model.

02

Menu Engineering & Food Cost

Recipe structure, portion discipline, yield awareness, ingredient utilization, pricing context, menu mix, and identifying items whose economics do not match their role.

03

Kitchen & Service Workflow

Prep systems, station flow, production sequencing, pars, handoffs, service timing, sanitation discipline, and practical recommendations for how work moves through the operation.

04

SOPs, Training & Standards

Repeatable procedures, recipe and plating standards, opening and closing systems, role clarity, training materials, accountability points, and consistency controls.

05

Procurement & Packaging Economics

Supplier and product comparisons, equivalent unit and case conversions, usage assumptions, packaging alternatives, equipment requirements, and practical sourcing decisions.

06

Location & Commercial Viability

Site history, surrounding businesses, demand generators, access, competition, trade areas, commercial assumptions, and evidence-based explanations for underperformance.

07

Revenue Channels & Market Development

Wholesale, hotels, weddings and events, corporate accounts, regional delivery, prospect qualification, account priorities, and repeat-order opportunities matched to the operation.

08

Opening, Relaunch & Implementation

Readiness planning, vendor coordination, operational resets, prioritized action plans, training, execution sequencing, and practical follow-through after the diagnostic.

Focused Business Diagnostic

When the source of the problem is unclear, a focused review can examine financial and operating inputs, menu mix, purchasing, labor, location conditions, guest access, competitive context, and commercial opportunities. Findings distinguish verified facts, client-provided information, working assumptions, and recommendations.

03 · Working Method

From question to execution.

Research is useful when it changes a decision. The process separates what is known from what needs validation, then prioritizes the actions that matter to the operation.

01

Observe

Understand the concept, operation, constraints, and owner objectives.

02

Investigate

Examine operating data, suppliers, geography, and relevant external evidence.

03

Prioritize

Rank options by commercial value, confidence, effort, and timing.

04

Implement

Turn findings into specific systems, outreach, sourcing, or operational changes.

05

Measure

Review execution and adjust against the engagement's agreed objectives.
